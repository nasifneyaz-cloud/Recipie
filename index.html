The problem

Waste is a planning problem, not a willpower problem

In 2022 the world wasted 1.05 billion tonnes of food. That is 132 kg for every person on the planet, around 19% of all the food that reached consumers.

Most of it did not go missing on farms or in supermarkets. Households threw away 631 million tonnes, 60% of the total, or 79 kg per person every year. That is at least a billion meals binned every day, while 783 million people were affected by hunger.

It heats the planet too. Food loss and waste produces roughly 8–10% of global greenhouse gas emissions.

So who is to blame? Not you, mostly. We shop without knowing what's already in the fridge. A week later we open the door, see half a cucumber, an egg and a heel of bread, and can't invent a meal from them. That is a gap in planning, and planning can be fixed.

Three odd ingredients are usually a meal. They just need someone to point at it.

    Storage habits

    Six small habits that make food last

    None of these take more than a minute. Together they stop a surprising amount of food from turning on you before you get to it.

    • Keep herbs in water, like flowers

      Trim the stems and stand soft herbs in a glass. Basil likes the counter. Parsley and coriander like the fridge with a loose bag over the top. They stay fresh for days longer than they do in the shop packet.

    • Freeze bread sliced

      Slice the loaf while it's fresh and freeze it flat. Toast slices straight from the freezer, one at a time, so the rest never goes stale on the counter.

    • Store onions away from potatoes

      Both like somewhere cool, dark and airy, just not side by side. Stored together they tend to spoil faster, so give each its own basket or shelf.

    • Move milk off the fridge door

      The door is the warmest part of the fridge and swings with every opening. Milk keeps longer on a middle shelf towards the back, where it stays colder.

    • Make an “eat me first” box

      Keep one box or shelf at eye level for anything near its date, half-used jars and leftovers. Look in it before you shop and cook from it first.

    • Keep a frozen scraps bag for stock

      Drop onion ends, carrot peelings, celery tops and mushroom stalks into a bag in the freezer. When it's full, simmer it in water for 45 minutes and strain. That's free stock.
